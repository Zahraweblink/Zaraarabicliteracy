Understanding Arabic Vowel Movements

In Arabic, letters carry short vowel marks called Haraakat which define their basic phonetic output. Extending these short vowels using Madd letters converts them into long vowels.

1 Short Vowel
َ

Fathah (فَتْحَة)

Sound: "a" (as in "cat")

A small diagonal line placed above a letter. It requires opening the mouth vertically to produce a short 'a' sound.

Anatomical Action: Open Jaw
2 Short Vowel
ِ

Kasrah (كَسْرَة)

Sound: "i" (as in "bit")

A small diagonal line placed below a letter. It requires lowering the bottom jaw slightly to emit a crisp 'i' sound.

Anatomical Action: Lower Jaw
3 Short Vowel
ُ

Dammah (ضَمَّة)

Sound: "u" (as in "put")

A small apostrophe-like symbol placed above a letter. It requires rounding and circling the lips.

Anatomical Action: Circle Lips

Long Vowels (Madd Letters)

Short vowels become long vowels (2 counts) when combined with their corresponding natural elongation letters:

ـَ + ا

Fathah + Alif = "aa"

ـِ + ي

Kasrah + Ya = "ee"

ـُ + و

Dammah + Waw = "oo"
