Understanding Mīm Sakinah (أَحْكَامُ المِيمِ السَّاكِنَةِ)

Mīm Sakinah (مْ) refers to a Mīm without a vowel sign. Depending on the letter immediately following it, the Mīm Sakinah takes one of three distinct rules, all governed by lip mechanics (*Shafawi*):

The 3 Rules of Mīm Sakinah: إِخْفَاء • إِدْغَام • إِظْهَار (Concealment • Merging • Clarity)
Breakdown of the 3 Rules

1. Ikhfa' Shafawi (إِخْفَاء شَفَوِي) — Concealment

Letter: ب (Baa)
When Mīm Sakinah is followed by Baa (ب), partially conceal the Mīm by gently closing the lips without heavy pressure, accompanying it with a 2-count nasal resonance (Ghunnah).

2. Idgham Shafawi (إِدْغَام شَفَوِي) — Merging

Letter: م (Meem)
When Mīm Sakinah is followed by another Mīm (م), merge them completely into a single doubled Mīm (مّ) with a strong 2-count Ghunnah.

3. Izhar Shafawi (إِظْهَار شَفَوِي) — Clarity

Letters: All remaining 26 Arabic letters.
Pronounce the Mīm Sakinah clearly and quickly with firm lip closure and no extra Ghunnah length.

⚠️ Warning: Major Pitfalls in Mīm Sakinah:

  • The Waaw (و) and Faa (ف) Danger Zone: Reciters often accidentally conceal (Ikhfa') Mīm before Waw or Faa because they share lip articulation. You must make them strictly Izhar (Clear)!
  • Pressing Lips Too Hard in Ikhfa': Tightening the lips destroys the gentle concealment quality of Ikhfa' Shafawi.
  • Omitting Ghunnah in Idgham: Merging two Mīms without holding the nasal hum for 2 counts.
