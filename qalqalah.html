Understanding Qalqalah (قَلْقَلَة)

Qalqalah literally means "shaking" or "echoing." In Tajweed, it refers to the sudden release or bounce of vocal pressure when pronouncing specific consonants carrying a Sukuun (سُكُون) or when pausing at the end of an Ayah.

The 5 Qalqalah Letters are in this historic phrase: قُطْبُ جَدٍّ (Qaf ق, Taa ط, Baa ب, Jeem ج, Dal د)
The 2 Main Degrees of Echo
1. Qalqalah Sughra (Minor)

Occurs when a Qalqalah letter is in the middle of a word with an explicit Sukuun. The echo is quick, light, and maintains continuous recitation flow.

يَقْطَعُونَ
2. Qalqalah Kubra (Major)

Occurs when stopping at the end of a word or Ayah on a Qalqalah letter. The bounce is pronounced, strong, and highly distinct.

الْفَلَقِ

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid:

  • Adding a Vowel (Fathah): Do not turn the bounce into a full vowel sound (e.g., saying "Yaj-a-loona" instead of "Yaj’loona").
  • Cutting the Echo Short: Releasing the air too abruptly without letting the natural echo reverberate off the articulation point.
  • Bouncing Non-Qalqalah Letters: Applying an echo to letters like Lām (ل) or Mīm (م) when they have Sukuun.
