Understanding Arabic Vowel Movements (Harakat) & Elongation (Madd)

In Arabic and Tajweed literacy, letters carry short vowel marks called Harakat which define their basic phonetic output without altering letter identity. Extending these short vowels using corresponding elongation letters transforms them into long vowels (Madd), which require precise duration control (2 counts).

1 Short Vowel
َ

Fathah (فَتْحَة)

Sound: "a" (as in "cat")

A small diagonal stroke placed above a letter. It requires opening the oral cavity vertically, dropping the lower jaw slightly to produce a crisp, short 'a' sound.

Anatomical Action: Open Jaw Vertically
2 Short Vowel
ِ

Kasrah (كَسْرَة)

Sound: "i" (as in "bit")

A small diagonal stroke placed below a letter. It requires pulling the corners of the lips slightly sideways and lowering the lower jaw to emit a clear 'i' sound.

Anatomical Action: Lower Jaw & Smile Corners
3 Short Vowel
ُ

Dammah (ضَمَّة)

Sound: "u" (as in "put")

A small comma-like symbol placed above a letter. It requires fully rounding and protruding the lips forward into a tight circular shape to produce the short 'u' sound.

Anatomical Action: Round & Protrude Lips

Long Vowels (Madd Natural / Al-Madd Al-Tabi'ee)

Short vowels are extended into long vowels spanning 2 counts (Harakat) when they precede their matching elongation letters:

ـَ + ا

Fathah + Alif = "aa"

Extends the 'a' sound with open jaw breath support for 2 beats.

ـِ + ي

Kasrah + Ya = "ee"

Extends the 'i' sound with horizontal lip spread for 2 beats.

ـُ + و

Dammah + Waw = "oo"

Extends the 'u' sound with sustained lip rounding for 2 beats.
