Detailed linguistic principles, triliteral root templates, and morphological patterns (Awzān).

فَعَلَ Past Verb (Fi'l Māḍī)

1. Triliteral Root Verb Base (الْفِعْلُ الْمَاضِي)

Explanation: The fundamental past-tense triliteral template (F-A-L) acting as the morphological cornerstone for Arabic word derivation.

Example Root & Word:

كَتَبَ (K-T-B) -> He wrote

Derived from the three radical consonants: Kāf, Tā', and Bā'.

فَاعِل Active Participle (Ism Fā'il)

2. Doer / Subject Noun (اِسْمُ الْفَاعِلِ)

Explanation: Formed on the pattern of Fā'il by inserting an alif after the first radical, designating the one who performs the action.

Example Root & Word:

كَاتِبٌ (Kātib) -> A writer / author

Created by applying the active participle template to the root K-T-B.

مَفْعُول Passive Participle (Ism Maf'ūl)

3. Object Noun (اِسْمُ الْمَفْعُولِ)

Explanation: Formed on the pattern of Maf'ūl by adding a prefixed Mīm and a wāw before the final radical, denoting the object acted upon.

Example Root & Word:

مَكْتُوبٌ (Maktūb) -> Written / A letter

Denotes that which has undergone the action of writing.

مَفْعَل Noun of Place / Time (Ism Makān/Zamān)

4. Noun of Place & Instrument (اِسْمُ الْمَكَانِ وَالْآلَةِ)

Explanation: Designates the geographical location or physical instrument where or with which an action takes place.

Example Root & Word:

مَكْتَبٌ (Maktab) -> Office / Desk

The place where writing is executed.
